Executive summary

Three conclusions

Pharma is paying today for validated targets in better formats. The more differentiated opportunity, and my main scientific interest, is approaches that restore immune homeostasis rather than block single mediators, provided they are validated in human disease.

1 · Validated targets, better formats

Pharma pays for convenience and combinability on proven pathways

Gilead paid $1.275B upfront for a Phase 1 oral IL-23R peptide in October 2026 (source); J&J's oral icotrokinra and its guselkumab + golimumab combination raise the bar further, and three anti-TL1A antibodies are in Phase 3 at once. Differentiation, not validation, is now the hard part. My judgment →

2 · Attractive biology often fails to translate

The negative evidence is as informative as the successes

An anti-OSMR antibody was stopped for futility in UC (NCT06137183), a TYK2 inhibitor missed in UC and Crohn's, and rituximab failed in UC. The mechanisms differ, but together they argue for human target validation in the right disease context. Full evidence in the appendix →

3 · Where room remains

Upstream sensing, immune homeostasis and different endpoints

RIPK2 inhibition targets microbial sensing upstream of TNF and IL-12/23, with randomised data due in 2027. Approaches that restore immune homeostasis are early but differentiated. Fibrostenotic Crohn's is judged on stricture length rather than remission and has no approved therapy. The RIPK2 case study →

My investment perspective

Validated targets versus immune reprogramming

Five themes; select one to read my reasoning. The overall judgment is below them.

1. Validated targets remain attractive, particularly with improved delivery

Recent pharmaceutical investment continues to favour clinically validated inflammatory pathways, including IL-23 and TL1A, with increasing emphasis on oral administration, longer dosing intervals and combination approaches. These strategies offer a clearer development rationale, although differentiation and competition are becoming increasingly important. I would prioritise programmes that deliver meaningful improvements in convenience without compromising efficacy or safety.

2. Novel immunomodulatory mechanisms could offer differentiated opportunities

Obefazimod (Abivax) is particularly interesting given its encouraging Phase 3 results in ulcerative colitis. Its proposed mechanism involves upregulation of miR-124, but I believe the precise molecular pathways responsible for its clinical effects warrant further investigation. The results nevertheless suggest that approaches beyond conventional cytokine blockade deserve serious attention.

3. Macrophage reprogramming is an area I would investigate further

My scientific interest lies in the role of macrophages in coordinating intestinal inflammation: they contribute to cytokine production, immune-cell recruitment and interactions with stromal cells involved in tissue remodelling and fibrosis. Rather than blocking individual mediators, I am interested in whether restoring homeostatic macrophage functions could reduce inflammatory signalling, limit pathological recruitment and support tissue repair at the same time.

This remains a hypothesis. The key questions are whether disease-associated macrophage states can be selectively and durably reprogrammed in human tissue, whether this improves clinically relevant outcomes, and whether essential host defence is preserved.

4. Immune-reprogramming startups merit continued monitoring

I would monitor companies developing macrophage-directed therapies, regulatory T-cell approaches and other immune-reprogramming strategies. Many remain early-stage, with substantial uncertainty around delivery, durability, safety and clinical translation. I would prioritise opportunities with convincing human disease biology, measurable pharmacodynamic biomarkers and a feasible path to clinical proof of concept.

5. Rational combination therapies may offer another route to deeper remission

IBD involves interacting inflammatory pathways, and inhibiting a single mechanism may not control disease in all patients. Combinations must demonstrate incremental benefit that justifies additional safety risk, treatment burden and cost; I would focus on those supported by complementary biology, biomarker-defined populations and a credible development strategy.

Overall judgment

I see two complementary opportunities in IBD: improving established mechanisms through differentiated drug formats and combinations, and identifying novel approaches capable of restoring immune homeostasis. My strongest scientific interest is in macrophage and broader immune reprogramming, but I would distinguish scientific promise from investment readiness, and advance opportunities only when human disease relevance, therapeutic differentiation and a credible development pathway justify further diligence.

Company case study

Accro Bioscience (Accropeutics): AC-101, an oral RIPK2 inhibitor

The company

Private, Suzhou. $50M Series C in May 2026 led by OrbiMed, with TCG Crossover and Lilly Asia Ventures. AC-101 has completed Phase 1 in Australia and China and a proof-of-concept Phase 1b/2a in Chinese UC patients. A randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 2b in moderate-to-severe UC dosed its first patient on 20 September 2026, with primary completion estimated for November 2027 (NCT07755241; Series C).

The competitive frame

Odyssey Therapeutics' OD-001 leads the class with open-label Phase 2a data (source) (27% clinical remission and 61% response at week 12, 20 mg twice daily) and randomised trials reading out in H2 2027. Oncodesign's OPM-101 has healthy-volunteer data only, and GSK stopped an earlier RIPK2 inhibitor on toxicology findings.

My diligence questions

Clinical differentiation: does AC-101 show convincing placebo-adjusted efficacy, dose response and a competitive safety profile? Mechanistic differentiation: is RIPK2 inhibition sufficiently selective, and can biomarkers identify likely responders? Commercial opportunity: are the intellectual property, global development strategy and valuation attractive relative to competitors?

My recommendation

Advance Accro to focused scientific and commercial diligence, with a view to participating in a future financing round ahead of the Phase 2b readout.

I would stop diligence if AC-101 shows inadequate target engagement at tolerable doses, fails to separate from placebo or to differentiate from OD-001 in Phase 2b, or if the financing terms or cross-border investment structure are unfavourable.
