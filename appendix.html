The headline the sweep found

A big-pharma OSMR antibody already failed in UC

Genentech's vixarelimab Phase 2 in moderate-to-severe UC (Moonglow, NCT06137183) was terminated after a futility analysis judged it unlikely to meet its primary endpoint. Vixarelimab targets OSMR, the receptor for oncostatin M; the best biomarker-validated stromal cytokine axis has been tried and has not worked in UC (remission 11.1% vs 11.5% placebo).

What a top-down map misses

Whole mechanism classes were invisible until the registry pull

Checkpoint agonists (rosnilimab, ALTB-268), a BCMA × CD19 CAR-T aimed at refractory IBD, a second fibrostenotic-Crohn's asset (ontunisertib), and a TNF × OX40L bispecific (brivekimig) all sit in registries and were absent from Map 1. Roughly a third of the new chips carry only a company code with no disclosed mechanism.

Limits: academic-sponsored trials are excluded; preclinical companies are invisible to a registry; trials started before 2023 are not in this pull; registry status can lag company news. Programme grouping and class assignment were automated and spot-checked, not hand-verified for every row.

A5 · Methodology

How this was built, and what it misses

Compiled from company releases, trial registries, peer-reviewed reports and conference abstracts. Figures are quoted as published; nothing was estimated or interpolated. Company-reported and conference figures are labelled as such. The claims behind the summary and framework were checked against primary sources on 28 October 2026 (listed under Key sources).

How AI was used. AI tools (Claude) were used to search for, extract and organise sources, run the trial-registry sweep and build this page. Mechanism classification, interpretation and the investment judgements are the author's, and material claims were checked by the author against the linked sources.

This is not a census, and it should not be read as one. A 2026 review counts over 100 investigational agents in Phase 2–3 for ulcerative colitis alone. The map covers mechanism classes and the programmes that define them. Known systematic holes: Map 1 was built without a trial-registry pull — Map 2 adds one (22 October), and its 84 new programmes are not yet folded into Map 1; the curated map underrepresents Chinese and Japanese pipelines, although the registry view captures many Chinese-sponsored trials; preclinical companies appear only where they reached the trade press; and no subscription pipeline database was used. An earlier version of this page understated its own gaps, and one of its claims — that no 2026 financing had gone to immune reprogramming in IBD — was withdrawn after a wider sweep found several.

Known gaps

  1. Tulisokibart's Phase 3 efficacy figures are topline only — the single most consequential missing number here.
  2. Phase 3 efficacy for duvakitug and afimkibart not found.
  3. Mirikizumab remission figures conflict across sources, using different endpoints and timepoints.
  4. Obefazimod's induction placebo-adjusted delta is reported four different ways across four sources.
  5. Coultreon's IBD relevance is unconfirmed; the company states autoimmune disease without naming IBD.
  6. SPY003 is confirmed as a half-life-extended anti-IL-23p19 antibody; SPY002's target is not yet verified against company materials.
  7. A ClinicalTrials.gov sweep was run on 22 October 2026 (Map 2): 286 trials, 104 programmes, 84 not on Map 1, and 32 of those with no disclosed mechanism. They are shown, not yet scored.
  8. Crohn's disease is underrepresented relative to ulcerative colitis throughout, reflecting where the literature sits. The one Crohn's benchmark so far is risankizumab's AFFIRM trial; Crohn's and UC remission figures use different scores and placebo rates and are not compared on the chart.
  9. Microbiome and barrier-repair classes are thin here and deserve their own pass.
  10. RXC008's Phase 1 pharmacokinetic figures were not retrievable, and first-patient dosing in ROCKETEER is not yet confirmed. The Skye–Redx combination into Fibrx still requires shareholder votes, UK High Court sanction and Nasdaq approval.
  11. Icotrokinra's Phase 2b now has a primary source (NEJM, 14 October 2026, n=252), but the matching placebo remission rate was not retrievable, which is why it stays off the chart.
  12. DUET figures are from conference coverage and a company release, not a peer-reviewed report.
  13. JNJ-78934804 appears in DDW coverage as a dual IL-23/TNF co-antibody and is probably the same asset as JNJ-4804 under another code. It is counted once, not twice.
  14. Sonoma Biotherapeutics, Abata and GentiBio run Treg programmes but no IBD indication was confirmed for any of them, so they are off the map rather than on it unverified.
  15. Mufemilast's sponsor was not identified. Mestag's M402 is described for inflammatory disease without IBD being named explicitly.
  16. The OSM/OSMR axis has strong biomarker validation in anti-TNF-refractory IBD, but Genentech's anti-OSMR antibody vixarelimab was terminated for futility in UC (NCT06137183): the axis has been tried and did not work in that setting.
